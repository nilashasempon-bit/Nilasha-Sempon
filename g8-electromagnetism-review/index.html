Starter: the 4 big questions of Lesson 4

These are the 4 investigation questions from your textbook. Say your answer to your partner, then tap to check.

1 · What affects the strength of a magnetic field around a current?
✓ More current (more voltage), winding the wire into a coil, more loops, and an iron core inside the coil.
2 · How can electric energy be used to create motion?
✓ In an electric motor. The poles of an electromagnet reverse when the current reverses, so the coil keeps turning between the magnets.
3 · How can motion be used to produce electrical energy?
✓ In an electric generator. Moving a magnet and a coil past each other makes a current, like in a bike light generator.
4 · What affects the strength of the current made by a magnet?
✓ A stronger magnet, more coils (loops), faster motion, and keeping the magnet perpendicular to the coil.

From your textbook
Read, then answer
Q1 Why does a compass needle move near a wire with a current?
✓ The magnetic field around the wire puts a force on the needle.
Q2 Where is the field strongest?
✓ Close to the wire. It gets weaker farther away.
Q3 Give two ways to make the field stronger.
✓ More current (more voltage) · wind the wire into a coil · more loops · more wires side by side.

Textbook: electromagnets

From your textbook
Read, then answer
Q1 What turns a coil into an electromagnet?
✓ An iron (magnetic) core inside the coil, with a current flowing.
Q2 Why can the crane drop the scrap metal?
✓ When the current is turned off, the electromagnet loses its magnetic field.
Q3 What happens to the poles when the current reverses?
✓ The north and south poles reverse (swap).

Textbook: electric motors

From your textbook
Read, then answer
Q1 Name the 3 main parts of a simple motor.
✓ A coil of wire on a rotating shaft · a permanent magnet · a source of electric energy (battery).
Q2 What does a commutator do?
✓ It is a switch that reverses the current in the coil.
Q3 What 3 things decide how strong a motor is?
✓ The strength of the permanent magnet · the voltage · the number of wire coils.

✓ There is no current in the circuit.

✓ The direction the magnet (or coil) moves.

✓ A generator makes AC (changes direction). A battery makes DC (one direction).

Critical thinking: claim · evidence · reasoning

🔍 Odd one out: which device does not need an electric motor?
Hint: which one never moves?
1 · Hamdan says: "A permanent magnet is better for the scrapyard crane." Do you agree?
✓ Disagree. A permanent magnet could never let go of the iron. An electromagnet can be turned off to drop it.
2 · Shamma's bike light gets brighter when she pedals faster. Why?
✓ Pedalling faster spins the magnet (or coil) faster. Faster motion makes a stronger current, so the light is brighter.
3 · Why does a motor need a commutator to keep spinning?
✓ Every half turn, the commutator reverses the current, so the coil's poles reverse. The magnets keep pushing it the same way, so it keeps turning.

Purple

  1. Explain why a generator makes AC, not DC.
  2. A magnet sits still inside a coil. Explain why the ammeter reads zero.

Answers
1. As the coil spins, its sides pass the N and S poles in turn, so the current changes direction each half turn.
2. Only a changing (moving) magnetic field makes a current. No motion means no current.
3. A motor changes electric energy into mechanical energy. A generator changes mechanical energy into electric energy.

Mystery solved!

Magnets and electricity working together

Shamma's bike light has a small GENERATOR. When she pedals, a magnet and a coil move past each other. This motion makes an electric current, so the light shines. No pedalling, no motion, no current! The crane uses an ELECTROMAGNET: a coil with a current around an iron core. Current on: it lifts the iron. Current off: the magnetic field goes away and the iron drops. This is electromagnetism.
