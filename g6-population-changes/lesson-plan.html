Success criteria

  • I can explain what overpopulation is and how it affects other populations.
  • I can give reasons why a population might decrease.
  • I can tell the difference between extinct, endangered and threatened species.
  • I can describe how people can help a population recover.

Key vocabulary

  • Overpopulation: When a population grows so large that it causes damage to the environment.
  • Population decrease: When the number of individuals in a population gets smaller.
  • Natural disaster: A flood, fire or volcanic eruption that can reduce populations.
  • Predation: When one animal hunts and eats another.
  • Extinct: A species that has died out: no individuals are left.
  • Endangered: A species whose population is at risk of extinction.
  • Critically endangered: Endangered species that face an even higher risk of extinction.
  • Threatened: A species at risk, but not yet endangered.

Differentiation

Green · Support

  1. Complete: “An ____ species has no individuals left.”
  2. Name one natural disaster.
  3. True or false: the mountain gorilla is extinct.
  4. Which UAE animal came back from extinction in the wild?
Answers: Answers
1. extinct
2. flood, fire or volcanic eruption
3. False (it is endangered)
4. the Arabian oryx

Orange · At level

  1. Explain how an overpopulation of birds affects spiders and lizards.
  2. Compare threatened and endangered species.
  3. Give two ways the UAE protects species at risk.
Answers: Answers
1. Birds eat more spiders, so spiders decrease; lizards that eat spiders then decrease too.
2. Both are at risk; endangered species are closer to extinction.
3. Any two: reserves (Sir Bani Yas), breeding centres (Sharjah), protected beaches (Saadiyat), protecting seagrass for dugongs.

Purple · Challenge

  1. Why is it harder to save a species when only a few individuals are left?
  2. Can overpopulation of one species cause another to become endangered? Explain.
  3. Plan a campaign to protect hawksbill turtles in the UAE.
Answers: Answers
1. Few individuals means few babies; one disaster or disease could wipe them all out.
2. Yes: if a predator overpopulates, it can eat so much prey that the prey becomes endangered.
3. e.g. protect nesting beaches, reduce plastic, turn off beach lights at night, teach others.
