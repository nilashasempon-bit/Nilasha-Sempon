Success criteria

  • Foundation: I can define epistasis and X inactivation and give an example of each.
  • Secure: I can work out Labrador coat colours from genotypes and explain the calico pattern.
  • Advanced: I can explain the 9 : 3 : 4 ratio and why calico cats are almost always female.

Key vocabulary

  • Epistasis: Two different genes interact to affect one trait; one gene hides the effect of another.
  • Autosomes: Chromosome pairs 1 to 22: not involved in sex determination.
  • Sex chromosomes: Pair 23: X and Y. Females XX, males XY.
  • X inactivation: One X chromosome is randomly switched off in each female cell.
  • Dosage compensation: Balancing X-gene dose between XX females and XY males by X inactivation.
  • Barr body: The small, dense, darkly stained inactive X chromosome.
  • Sex-linked trait: A trait controlled by a gene on a sex chromosome (mostly on X).
  • Gamete: A mature sex cell (sperm or egg) with a haploid number of chromosomes.

Differentiation

Green · Mandatory ★

  1. Which could be the genotype of a chocolate Labrador: Eebb, EEbb, eeBb, EEBB?
  2. What is the name of the process that makes one X inactive in female mammals?
Answers: Answers
1. Eebb and EEbb.
2. Dosage compensation (X inactivation).

Blue · Less mandatory ★★

  1. Test cross: EeBb × eebb. Give the phenotype ratio.
  2. Explain why the inactive X looks like a dark spot (Barr body) in a female nucleus.
Answers: Answers
1. EeBb, Eebb, eeBb, eebb → 1 black : 1 chocolate : 2 yellow.
2. The inactive X is tightly condensed (packed), so it stains darkly.

Orange · Optional ★★★

  1. Explain why EeBb × EeBb gives 9 : 3 : 4, not 9 : 3 : 3 : 1.
  2. A male calico cat is found. Suggest his sex chromosomes and explain.
Answers: Answers
1. The 3 eeB_ and 1 eebb dogs are all yellow because ee masks B, so 3 + 1 = 4 yellow.
2. XXY: two X chromosomes (XOXB), one randomly inactivated in each cell.
