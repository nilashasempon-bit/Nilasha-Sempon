Lesson plan · Reflection, Absorption and Transmission

🎯 Learning objective: Describe how waves are reflected, absorbed and transmitted by matter.

Success criteria

  • I can define reflection and explain that a wave reflects at the same angle it strikes a barrier.
  • I can define absorption and explain why soft, porous materials absorb more sound energy.
  • I can define transmission and give examples of waves passing through a medium.
  • I can explain how acoustical engineers and highway sound barriers control sound.

Key vocabulary

  • Reflection: The bouncing of a wave off a surface. All waves reflect.
  • Echo: A sound wave that has reflected off a surface and comes back to you.
  • Absorption: The transfer of energy by a wave to the medium through which it travels.
  • Transmission: The passage of a wave through a medium.
  • Barrier: An object or surface that a wave runs into.
  • Angle of reflection: Always equal to the angle at which the wave strikes the barrier.
  • Insulation: Soft material inside walls that absorbs sound energy.
  • Acoustical engineer: An engineer who designs spaces and materials to control sound.

Differentiation

Green · Support

  1. Complete: “The bouncing of a wave off a surface is ____.”
  2. Circle: foam absorbs / reflects sound.
  3. True or false: an echo is a reflected sound wave.
  4. Which lets more sound through: a thin door or a thick concrete wall?
Answers: Answers
1. reflection
2. absorbs
3. True
4. a thin door

Orange · At level

  1. Draw a wave hitting a barrier at 40°. Draw the normal and label both angles.
  2. Why do recording studios cover their walls with foam?
  3. Give one example each of reflection, absorption and transmission in your home.
Answers: Answers
1. Both angles are 40°, measured from the normal; the reflected wave mirrors the incoming one.
2. Foam is soft and porous, so it absorbs sound energy instead of reflecting it as echoes.
3. e.g. an echo in an empty room; a carpet quietening footsteps; hearing the TV through a wall.

Purple · Challenge

  1. You shout at a cliff and hear the echo 2 s later. Sound travels at 343 m/s. How far away is the cliff?
  2. Design a quiet study room. Which materials would you use for the walls, floor and door? Explain.
  3. Why are highway barriers built higher near homes, and from hard materials?
Answers: Answers
1. 343 × 2 = 686 m there and back, so the cliff is 343 m away.
2. Carpet, curtains and foam panels absorb sound; an insulated wall and a solid door reduce transmission.
3. A higher wall blocks and reflects more sound; hard surfaces reflect noise back towards the road instead of transmitting it.
