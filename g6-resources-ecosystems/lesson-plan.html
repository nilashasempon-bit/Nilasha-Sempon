Success criteria

  • I can tell the difference between living and nonliving things in an environment.
  • I can define species, population and community, and give an example of each.
  • I can put the five levels of organization in order, from individual to biosphere.
  • I can explain which resources living things get from their ecosystem.

Key vocabulary

  • Individual: A single member of a species, like one elephant.
  • Species: A group of organisms with similar traits that can produce fertile offspring.
  • Population: All the organisms of the same species living in the same area at the same time.
  • Community: All the populations of different species living together in the same area at the same time.
  • Ecosystem: All the living and nonliving things in an area.
  • Biosphere: All the ecosystems on Earth: the parts of Earth and its atmosphere where there is life.
  • Living (biotic): Things that grow, need energy and reproduce: animals, plants, fungi, bacteria.
  • Nonliving (abiotic): Things that are not alive: water, rocks, soil, sunlight, air.

Differentiation

Green · Support

  1. Complete: “A single member of a species is an ____.”
  2. Circle the nonliving thing: oryx / sand / ghaf tree.
  3. True or false: a population has many different species.
  4. Which is bigger: a community or an ecosystem?
Answers: Answers
1. individual
2. sand
3. False (only one species)
4. an ecosystem

Orange · At level

  1. Draw five boxes from individual to biosphere. Add a UAE example in each.
  2. Explain the difference between a population and a community.
  3. List three resources an Arabian oryx gets from its ecosystem.
Answers: Answers
1. e.g. one oryx → all oryx on Sir Bani Yas → oryx, gazelles and ghaf trees → + sun, water, sand → Earth.
2. A population is one species; a community is all the populations of different species in an area.
3. Food (grasses), water, shelter (shade of trees), space.

Purple · Challenge

  1. A horse and a donkey can have a mule, but mules cannot reproduce. Are horses and donkeys one species? Explain.
  2. Predict what happens to the flamingo population if Al Wathba's water dries up.
  3. Design a fair survey to compare the ant populations in two parts of the school garden.
Answers: Answers
1. No: their offspring (the mule) is not fertile, so they are different species.
2. It would fall: flamingos lose water, food (shrimp, algae) and shelter, so they die or move away.
3. Same size quadrat, same time of day, several throws in each area; count ants and compare the averages.
