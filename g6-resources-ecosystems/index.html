How to tell · living things…
  • grow and develop
  • need food or energy
  • reproduce (make more of their kind)
  • respond to their surroundings
Tricky! Something that was once alive, like a fallen dead branch, is no longer living.
Why? Trees, oryx, mangroves and flamingos grow, need energy and reproduce: they are living. Sand, sunlight, sea water and rocks are nonliving, but living things need them!
Advanced fact: scientists call living parts biotic and nonliving parts abiotic.

Textbook definition

Living things are organized in levels: individual → population → community → ecosystem → biosphere. All the ecosystems on Earth make up the biosphere.

Resources: what does an ecosystem give?

Living things get the resources they need from the living and nonliving parts of their ecosystem. Think of an example for each, then tap Reveal.

🍃 Food

🐐 → 🌿
energy to live
The oryx eats desert grasses and ghaf leaves. Flamingos eat tiny shrimp and algae.

💧 Water

🦩 → 💧
a nonliving resource
Every living thing needs water. Wetlands like Al Wathba give birds water and food.

🏠 Shelter

🐟 → 🌳
a safe place
Mangrove roots in Abu Dhabi shelter young fish and crabs. Ghaf trees give shade to animals.

☀️ Sunlight, air & space

🌱 → ☀️
room to grow
Plants need sunlight and air to make food. Animals need space to find food and raise young.
UAE sustainability link: the UAE protects reserves like Al Wathba and Sir Bani Yas so every population keeps the food, water, shelter and space it needs.

Mystery solved!

How one flamingo is part of the whole planet

Shamma's flamingo is an INDIVIDUAL. All the flamingos at Al Wathba are a POPULATION. Add the fish, shrimp and plants and you have a COMMUNITY. Add the water, mud and sunshine and it becomes an ECOSYSTEM. And all the ecosystems on Earth together make the BIOSPHERE. One bird, five levels, one planet!
