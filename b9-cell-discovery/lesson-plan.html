Success criteria

  • Foundation: I can state the three ideas of the cell theory and name the scientists who discovered cells.
  • Secure: I can compare light and electron microscopes (TEM and SEM) and calculate total magnification.
  • Advanced: I can explain how better microscopes led to the cell theory and why resolution matters.

Key vocabulary

  • Cell: The basic structural and functional unit of all living things.
  • Cell theory: All living things are made of cells; the cell is the basic unit of life; cells come from pre-existing cells.
  • Microscope: An instrument that magnifies things too small to see with the eye.
  • Compound light microscope: Uses glass lenses and visible light; magnifies up to about 1000×.
  • Electron microscope: Uses a beam of electrons; magnifies up to about 500,000×.
  • Magnification: How large an image appears compared with its actual size.
  • Resolution: The clarity and detail of an image.
  • TEM and SEM: Transmission (2D, inside) and scanning (3D, surface) electron microscopes.

Differentiation

Foundation ★

  1. State the three ideas of the cell theory.
  2. Who first saw bacteria?
Answers: Answers
1. All living things are made of cells; the cell is the basic unit of life; cells come from cells.
2. Leeuwenhoek.

Secure ★★

  1. Compare light and electron microscopes.
  2. Find the total magnification of a 10× eyepiece and a 40× objective.
Answers: Answers
1. Light: lenses, about 1000×, living cells. Electron: electron beam, up to 500,000×, dead specimens.
2. 10 × 40 = 400×.

Advanced ★★★

  1. Explain why the cell theory needed microscopes.
  2. Explain why viruses are studied with electron microscopes.
Answers: Answers
1. Cells are too small to see by eye; microscopes gave the evidence.
2. Viruses are too small for light to resolve; electron beams give far higher resolution.
