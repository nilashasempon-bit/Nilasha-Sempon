Success criteria

  • I can measure wavelength from crest to crest, trough to trough, or compression to compression.
  • I can calculate frequency in hertz (Hz) as the number of waves ÷ time.
  • I can explain that as the frequency of a wave increases, its wavelength decreases.
  • I can explain that a higher frequency gives a higher pitch, and compare ranges of hearing.

Key vocabulary

  • Wavelength: Distance from one point on a wave to the same point on the next wave.
  • Frequency: The number of waves passing a point each second.
  • Hertz (Hz): The unit of frequency: 1 Hz = 1 wave per second.
  • Pitch: How high or low a sound seems.
  • Crest: The highest point of a transverse wave.
  • Compression: Where particles in a longitudinal wave are squeezed together.
  • Ultrasound: Sound above about 20,000 Hz: too high for humans to hear.
  • Infrasound: Sound below about 20 Hz: too low for humans to hear.

Differentiation

Green · Support

  1. Complete: “Frequency is measured in ____.”
  2. Circle: a thin oud string has a higher / lower pitch.
  3. True or false: wavelength can be measured from crest to crest.
  4. Higher pitch: a mosquito or a ship's horn?
Answers: Answers
1. hertz (Hz)
2. higher
3. True
4. a mosquito

Orange · At level

  1. Draw a low- and a high-frequency wave over the same time. Label one wavelength on each.
  2. A spring makes 30 waves in 10 s. What is its frequency?
  3. Why does a thick oud string sound lower than a thin one?
Answers: Answers
1. The high-frequency wave has more crests in the same width, so each wavelength is shorter.
2. 30 ÷ 10 = 3 Hz.
3. It vibrates more slowly: lower frequency, longer wavelength, so a lower pitch.

Purple · Challenge

  1. Textbook ratio: 1 wave every 4 s is 1:4. What is the ratio for 2 waves in 12 s? What is the frequency?
  2. Dolphins in UAE waters use ultrasound up to about 150,000 Hz. Why can't divers hear it?
  3. Design a test: does a shorter string give a higher pitch? What do you change, keep the same and measure?
Answers: Answers
1. 2:12 = 1:6, so 1 wave every 6 s. Frequency = 1 ÷ 6 ≈ 0.17 Hz.
2. It is far above 20,000 Hz, the top of the human hearing range, so our ears cannot detect it.
3. Change: string length. Keep: string type, tightness, how you pluck. Measure: frequency with a tuner app, 3 times each.
