Success criteria

  • I can use the speed of sound table to compare how fast sound travels in gases, liquids and solids.
  • I can explain how density and temperature change the speed of sound.
  • I can define diffraction and explain when it is greatest.
  • I can name the parts of the ear and describe how a sound wave travels through them.

Key vocabulary

  • Speed of sound: How fast sound travels: about 343 m/s in air at 20°C.
  • Medium: The material a wave travels through: a gas, liquid or solid.
  • Density: How closely packed the particles of a material are.
  • Temperature: How hot or cold a medium is. It changes how fast particles move.
  • Diffraction: The change in direction of a wave at the edge of an object or through an opening.
  • Eardrum: A thin membrane that vibrates when sound waves hit it.
  • Hammer, anvil, stirrup: Three tiny bones in the middle ear that pass vibrations on.
  • Cochlea: The snail-shaped, fluid-filled part of the inner ear.

Differentiation

Green · Support

  1. Complete: “Sound travels fastest through ____.”
  2. Circle: sound is faster in warm / cold air.
  3. True or false: sound can bend around corners.
  4. Is sound faster in water or in air?
Answers: Answers
1. solids
2. warm
3. True (diffraction)
4. water

Orange · At level

  1. Use the table: about how many times faster is sound in iron than in air at 20°C?
  2. Why can you hear people in a room before you reach its door?
  3. Describe the path of sound from the ear canal to the cochlea.
Answers: Answers
1. 5,130 ÷ 343 ≈ 15 times faster.
2. Sound diffracts through the doorway and spreads into the hallway.
3. Ear canal → eardrum vibrates → hammer, anvil, stirrup → fluid in the cochlea.

Purple · Challenge

  1. You hear thunder 6 s after the lightning. Sound travels at 343 m/s. How far away is the storm?
  2. Why does sound diffract around a doorway, but light does not?
  3. Why does heating speed up sound in a gas, but cooling speeds it up in a liquid?
Answers: Answers
1. 6 × 343 ≈ 2,058 m, about 2 km.
2. Sound wavelengths are similar to a doorway's size; light wavelengths are far smaller.
3. Hot gas particles move faster and collide more; cooled liquid molecules move closer together and collide more often.
