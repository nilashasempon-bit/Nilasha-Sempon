Success criteria

  • Foundation: I can explain why stomata close in hot, dry conditions and name examples of C4 and CAM plants.
  • Secure: I can describe how C4 plants separate the steps in space and CAM plants separate them in time.
  • Advanced: I can explain photorespiration and evaluate which strategy suits the UAE best.

Key vocabulary

  • Stomata: Tiny pores in the leaf that let CO₂ in and water vapour out.
  • Photorespiration: Rubisco fixes O₂ instead of CO₂: no glucose is made and energy is wasted.
  • Mesophyll: Leaf cells where C4 plants first fix CO₂.
  • Bundle sheath: Cells around the veins where C4 plants run the Calvin cycle.
  • Oxaloacetate: The 4-carbon compound C4 plants make when they fix CO₂.
  • Spatial separation: Steps happen in different places (cells): the C4 strategy.
  • CAM: Crassulacean acid metabolism: stomata open at night; steps separated in time.
  • Vacuole: Where CAM plants store CO₂ as organic acids overnight.

Differentiation

Green · Mandatory ★

  1. Why do stomata close on hot days?
  2. Name two C4 and two CAM plants.
Answers: Answers
1. To conserve water.
2. C4: maize, sugarcane (grasses). CAM: pineapple, cacti (jade).

Blue · Less mandatory ★★

  1. Describe the C4 pathway in three steps.
  2. Describe what a CAM plant does at night and in the day.
Answers: Answers
1. Mesophyll fixes CO₂ into 4-C → moves to bundle sheath → CO₂ released into the Calvin cycle.
2. Night: stomata open, CO₂ stored as organic acids. Day: stomata closed, CO₂ released to the Calvin cycle.

Orange · Optional ★★★

  1. Explain how photorespiration wastes energy.
  2. Which strategy is better for the UAE? Justify your answer.
Answers: Answers
1. Rubisco fixes O₂ instead of CO₂, so no glucose is made but ATP and NADPH are used.
2. CAM saves the most water in the open desert; C4 grows faster for irrigated crops. Any justified answer.
