🖥️ Hot day leaf

SimulationClose the stomata: photorespirationCritical ThinkingNumeracy Skills
Questions to discuss (with answers)
  • What happens to CO₂ and O₂ as stomata close? → CO₂ falls (no new CO₂ enters); O₂ from the light reactions builds up.
  • Why does photorespiration rise? → Rubisco grabs O₂ more often when O₂ is high and CO₂ is low.
  • Why does the C4 leaf do better? → It pumps CO₂ into the bundle sheath, so rubisco there always has plenty of CO₂.

🧪 Stomata peel lab

LabOpen or closed?Numeracy SkillsTeamwork

You need: a grass or maize leaf (C4) · a succulent leaf, e.g. jade (CAM) · clear nail varnish · clear tape · slides · a light microscope

Method

  1. Paint a small patch of nail varnish on the underside of each leaf. Let it dry.
  2. Press clear tape on the patch, peel it off and stick it to a slide.
  3. Observe at 400×. Count the stomata in your field of view and note if they look open or closed.
  4. Compare the two leaves (sampled during the school day).

Safety: use nail varnish in a ventilated room, away from flames. Carry the microscope with two hands.
