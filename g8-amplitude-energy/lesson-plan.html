Lesson plan · Amplitude and Energy

🎯 Learning objective: Describe amplitude and explain how it is linked to the energy of a wave.

Success criteria

  • I can define amplitude and measure it from the rest position to a crest or trough.
  • I can describe what high and low amplitude look like in transverse and longitudinal waves.
  • I can explain the direct relationship: as amplitude increases, energy increases.
  • I can use amplitude to explain loudness and the energy of storm waves, tsunamis and earthquakes.

Key vocabulary

  • Amplitude: The maximum distance a wave moves from its rest position.
  • Rest position: Where the medium sits when no wave passes.
  • Crest: The highest point of a transverse wave.
  • Trough: The lowest point of a transverse wave.
  • Energy: The ability to cause change. It increases as amplitude increases.
  • Direct relationship: When one quantity increases, the other increases too.
  • Loudness: How loud a sound is. Higher amplitude = louder sound.
  • Decibel (dB): The unit used to measure loudness of sound.

Differentiation

Green · Support

  1. Complete: “Amplitude is measured from the ____ position.”
  2. Circle: a big wave carries more / less energy.
  3. True or false: a shout has a higher amplitude than a whisper.
  4. More energy: a gentle ripple or a storm wave?
Answers: Answers
1. rest
2. more
3. True
4. a storm wave

Orange · At level

  1. Draw a low- and a high-amplitude transverse wave. Label the amplitude on each.
  2. Why can a very loud sound make a window rattle?
  3. Describe a high- and a low-amplitude longitudinal wave on a spring.
Answers: Answers
1. Both have the same rest line; the arrow from rest to crest is short on the low wave and long on the high wave.
2. A loud sound has a high amplitude, so it carries more energy, enough to make the glass vibrate.
3. High: tightly packed compressions and very spread rarefactions. Low: coils only slightly squeezed.

Purple · Challenge

  1. Wave A has an amplitude of 2 m and wave B 4 m. Which carries more energy? Is it exactly twice as much?
  2. Airport ground crews wear ear defenders. Explain using amplitude and energy.
  3. Design a test: does dropping a ball from higher make taller ripples in a tray of water? Change? Keep? Measure?
Answers: Answers
1. Wave B. Doubling the amplitude gives about 4 times the energy, not just twice (energy grows faster than amplitude).
2. Jet engines make very high-amplitude sound that carries lots of energy and can damage the ear; defenders absorb it.
3. Change: drop height. Keep: ball, tray, water depth. Measure: ripple height with a ruler or video (repeat 3 times).
