Energy grows fast

The rule
E ∝ A2

Energy is proportional to the square of the amplitude.

Amplitude doubles (×2) → energy ×4
Amplitude halves (÷2) → energy ÷4 (one quarter)

Small changes in amplitude make BIG changes in energy.

Encounter: low or high amplitude?

Why? Loud sounds have a HIGH amplitude and carry MORE energy. Soft sounds have a LOW amplitude and carry less energy.
Direct relationship: as amplitude goes up, energy goes up.

What is amplitude? (the unconventional version)

Think of it like this

Think of a swing at the park. A bigger push makes the swing go higher from its resting point. It took more energy, and it carries more energy. A wave is the same: the higher it swings from rest, the more energy it has.

Textbook definition

The amplitude of a wave is the maximum distance the wave moves from its rest position.

Amplitude lab: small and big shakes

You need: a coil spring toy or a skipping rope · a small piece of ribbon or tape · the floor
  1. Lay the spring/rope on the floor. Tie the ribbon to the middle.
  2. Holder: keep one end still. Shaker: move the other end side to side once.
  3. Observer: how far does the ribbon move from its rest position? That is the amplitude.
  4. Repeat with a small then a big movement. Record below.

Decibels and sound energy

How much more energy passes through one square metre near the stage than far away?

Hint: every +10 dB multiplies the energy by 10. Try again!
🔒Answer correctly to unlock the decibel scale
70 → 80 dB: energy ×10
80 → 90 dB: energy ×10 again
10 × 10 = 100 times more energy near the stage

  1. Draw a low- and a high-amplitude transverse wave. Label the amplitude on each.
  2. Why can a very loud sound make a window rattle?
  3. Describe a high- and a low-amplitude longitudinal wave on a spring.
Answers
1. Both have the same rest line; the arrow from rest to crest is short on the low wave and long on the high wave.
2. A loud sound has a high amplitude, so it carries more energy, enough to make the glass vibrate.
3. High: tightly packed compressions and very spread rarefactions. Low: coils only slightly squeezed.

Purple · Challenge

Answers
1. Wave B. Doubling the amplitude gives about 4 times the energy, not just twice (energy grows faster than amplitude).
2. Jet engines make very high-amplitude sound that carries lots of energy and can damage the ear; defenders absorb it.
3. Change: drop height. Keep: ball, tray, water depth. Measure: ripple height with a ruler or video (repeat 3 times).

Mystery solved!

Why the shamal waves flattened the sandcastle

The strong shamal wind transferred more energy to the sea, so the waves rose much further from the rest position: a higher AMPLITUDE. Amplitude and energy have a direct relationship, so these TALL WAVES carried much more ENERGY to the beach than the calm, low-amplitude waves, enough to knock the sandcastle down!
