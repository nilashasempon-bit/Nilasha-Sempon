Activity 1: who does what in the membrane?

Is each clue about a PHOSPHOLIPID, a PROTEIN, CHOLESTEROL or a CARBOHYDRATE? Tap to choose (tap again to change), then check.

Why? Phospholipids make the bilayer. Proteins transport substances, receive signals and give support. Cholesterol stops the fatty-acid tails sticking together and keeps the membrane flexible. Carbohydrates are attached to proteins on the outside and give the cell its identity.

Activity 2: the border post (the unconventional version)

Think of it like this

The plasma membrane is like the UAE border. The wall and fence keep the inside separate. Gates let approved things through. Officers read signals and passports. Every traveller wears an ID badge, and flexible supports keep the fence from cracking.

Textbook definition

The plasma membrane is a thin outer covering found in all cells. It separates the inside of the cell from the outside environment, and is made mostly of phospholipids and proteins, with cholesterol and carbohydrates.

Textbook: the phospholipid bilayer

From your lesson
Remember: polar heads face outside (towards the water), nonpolar tails face inside (away from water).
Tap each idea to reveal it
IDEA 1 · What is a bilayer?
Two layers of phospholipids arranged so the membrane can exist in a watery environment.
IDEA 2 · Where do the heads and tails point?
The polar heads face the water on both sides; the nonpolar tails face each other in the middle.
IDEA 3 · Why does this matter?
This arrangement keeps the internal and external environments of the cell separate.

Proteins, cholesterol and carbohydrates

Watch: cell membrane structure and function
Read, then answer
Q1 Where are the proteins found, and what are three of their jobs?
✓ Embedded in the bilayer (some go all the way through, others sit on the surface). Receptors transmit signals, transport proteins move substances and wastes, others give support.
Q2 What does cholesterol do?
✓ It prevents the fatty-acid tails from sticking together and contributes to the fluidity (flexibility) of the membrane.
Q3 Where are carbohydrates found, and what do they do?
✓ Attached to proteins, sticking out from the outer surface. They define the cell's characteristics (identity) and help cells identify chemical signals.

Which arrangement lets the membrane exist in water?
Think about it

Heads (●) are polar and love water. Tails (〰) are nonpolar and avoid water. There is water outside the cell and water inside the cytoplasm.

Why is a single layer not enough?
✓ One side of the tails would touch water. In a bilayer, both sides have heads touching water and the tails are hidden.
UAE innovation link: the UAE makes most of its drinking water by desalination, using membranes that let water through but hold back salt. Scientists learned from the selective plasma membrane!

Exit ticket: why is it the gatekeeper of the cell?

Mystery solved!

The plasma membrane is a PHOSPHOLIPID BILAYER: polar heads face the water on both sides and nonpolar tails hide in the middle, so it does not dissolve and keeps inside and outside separate. PROTEINS act as gates and signal receivers, CHOLESTEROL keeps it flexible, and CARBOHYDRATES are its ID badges. Like the officers at Hatta, it controls what enters and leaves: the gatekeeper of the cell.
