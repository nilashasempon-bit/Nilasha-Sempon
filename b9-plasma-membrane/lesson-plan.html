Success criteria

  • Foundation: I can name the four components of the plasma membrane and the three parts of a phospholipid.
  • Secure: I can describe the phospholipid bilayer: polar heads face water, nonpolar tails face inward.
  • Advanced: I can explain the roles of proteins, cholesterol and carbohydrates, and why the bilayer lets the membrane exist in water.

Key vocabulary

  • Semipermeable: Lets some substances pass through but not others.
  • Phospholipid: A lipid with a polar head and two nonpolar tails; the main part of membranes.
  • Glycerol: The backbone molecule that holds the phosphate group and the fatty acids together.
  • Phosphate group: The polar, water-loving part of the phospholipid head.
  • Fatty acid: A long nonpolar chain; each phospholipid has two fatty-acid tails.
  • Protein: Embedded in the bilayer: transport, receptor and support jobs.
  • Cholesterol: Stops the fatty-acid tails from sticking together.
  • Carbohydrates: Chains attached to proteins on the outside; they give the cell its identity.

Differentiation

Green · Mandatory ★

  1. Name the four components of the plasma membrane.
  2. Label a phospholipid: head, tails, glycerol, phosphate.
Answers: Answers
1. Phospholipids, proteins, cholesterol, carbohydrates.
2. Head = phosphate group (polar) with glycerol; tails = two fatty acids (nonpolar).

Blue · Less mandatory ★★

  1. Describe the phospholipid bilayer.
  2. Give two jobs of membrane proteins. Then try the Wordwall diagram.
Answers: Answers
1. Two layers of phospholipids: polar heads face water outside and inside, nonpolar tails face inward.
2. Transport substances; receive signals (receptors); support the membrane.

Orange · Optional ★★★

  1. Explain why the membrane would fall apart without its bilayer structure.
  2. Explain the jobs of cholesterol and carbohydrates.
Answers: Answers
1. Nonpolar tails would touch water; the bilayer hides them and keeps inside and outside separate.
2. Cholesterol stops tails sticking together (flexibility); carbohydrates give identity and help identify chemical signals.
