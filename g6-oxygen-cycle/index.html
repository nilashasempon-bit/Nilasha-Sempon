Hook: the Hatta air mystery

A winter weekend at Hatta Dam…

Shamma kayaks hard across the dam while her family lights a campfire on the shore. She stops, out of breath, and has a question.

“We are all using oxygen: me, the fish, the fire, every visitor. Why doesn't it run out?”

Thousands of living things use oxygen every second. So where does new oxygen come from?

Predict: what makes oxygen, and what uses it? Talk with your partner for 1 minute.
Clue: look at the trees around the dam and the green algae in the water.

Encounter: uses or makes oxygen?

Tap each example: does it mainly use or make oxygen? Tap again to change.

Why? Plants and algae MAKE oxygen by photosynthesis. Animals, people and fires USE oxygen and release carbon dioxide.
Advanced fact: plants respire too, but in sunlight they make much more oxygen than they use.

The great gas swap (the unconventional version)

Think of it like this

Think of the oxygen cycle as a gas swap. Like friends swapping cards in the majlis: plants hand oxygen to animals, and animals hand carbon dioxide back. Nothing runs out!

Textbook definition

✓ They need it for cellular respiration. It is also part of substances like carbon dioxide and water.
Q2 How did oxygen first get into Earth's atmosphere?
✓ Bacteria that could photosynthesise evolved. Oxygen was a by-product, so it entered the air. Later, more photosynthetic organisms added more.

From your textbook

Read, then answer
Q1 Which two cycles are linked in this paragraph?
✓ The carbon cycle and the oxygen cycle.
Q2 How are they linked?
✓ Photosynthesis takes in CO₂ and gives out O₂; respiration does the opposite. The same carbon and oxygen atoms take different forms and play a role in both cycles.

Oxygen in water: day and night

Fish and other water animals use dissolved oxygen: oxygen gas mixed into the water from water plants and algae and from the air at the surface.

Wind, rain and splashing mix more air into water. Think of the kayakers at Hatta Dam stirring up the water.

Predict, then tap to check
They stop. No light means no photosynthesis, so the pondweed makes no new oxygen.
Little new oxygen. Plants make no oxygen in the dark, and still water mixes in little air, so fish can run short of oxygen.

Oxygen detective: where is oxygen hiding?

Oxygen is not only in the air. It is part of rocks, water and living things too. Spot the red O atoms in each picture, then tap Reveal.

In rocks

sand, rust, minerals
Oxygen is locked in sand (SiO₂), rocks and rust (iron oxide).

In water

H₂O + dissolved O₂
Water is H₂O. Fish use the oxygen gas dissolved in it.

In air

about 21% O₂
About 21% of air is O₂. CO₂ has oxygen too. Most of the air is nitrogen (N₂).

In living things

about 65% of you
Your body is about 65% oxygen by mass: in water, sugar and proteins.
UAE space link: Emirati astronauts on the space station breathe oxygen made by splitting water (H₂O) with electricity.

You need: pondweed (Elodea) · a beaker of water · a lamp · a ruler · a stopwatch

Mystery solved!

Why Hatta never runs out of oxygen

People, animals, fish and campfires use oxygen and give out carbon dioxide through RESPIRATION and burning. But trees, date palms and the algae in Hatta Dam take in that carbon dioxide and, through PHOTOSYNTHESIS, release new oxygen. Oxygen moves round and round in a CYCLE, so it is used and replaced all the time!
