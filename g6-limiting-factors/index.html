Hook: Grandpa's pond mystery

A weekend at Grandpa's farm in Al Ain…

Shamma helps her grandfather feed the tilapia fish in his farm pond. “When I started, there were only 4 fish,” he says. “They had babies and the pond filled up fast. But for a whole year there have been about 16 fish. No more!”

Shamma wonders: “Why did the fish stop increasing?”

What could be stopping the population from growing?

Predict: list everything the fish need to survive. Which one might run out first? Talk with your partner for 1 minute.
Clue: think about the pond's size, the food and the oxygen in the water. Can they grow too?

Encounter: living or nonliving limiting factor?

Each of these can limit a population. Tap each one: is it a living or a NONLIVING factor? Tap again to change.

Why? Food plants, algae, predators and shelter trees are living factors. Water, temperature, space and sunlight are nonliving factors. Both kinds can limit a population!

The school bus (the unconventional version)

Think of it like this

An ecosystem is like a school bus with 40 seats. More and more students get on, but when all the seats are full, no more can fit. The seats are the limiting factor, and 40 is the bus's carrying capacity!

Textbook definition

A limiting factor is anything that restricts the size of a population. Carrying capacity is the largest number of individuals of one species that an ecosystem can support over time.

How does each limiting factor affect a population? Predict, then tap the card to check!

Mystery solved!

Why Grandpa's pond stopped at 16 fish

At first the pond had plenty of RESOURCES, so the 4 fish had babies and the population grew fast. But the pond's space, food and oxygen are LIMITING FACTORS: they do not grow when the fish do. So the population levelled off at the pond's CARRYING CAPACITY: about 16 fish. It never reached its BIOTIC POTENTIAL!
