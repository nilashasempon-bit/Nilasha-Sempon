Success criteria

  • Foundation: I can state where the Calvin cycle happens and name its inputs (CO₂, ATP, NADPH) and product (G3P).
  • Secure: I can describe the three phases: carbon fixation, reduction and regeneration of RuBP.
  • Advanced: I can explain how NADPH's electrons reduce 3-PGA to G3P, and compare the C4 and CAM pathways.

Key vocabulary

  • Light-independent phase: The Calvin cycle: uses ATP and NADPH to turn CO₂ into sugar.
  • Stroma: The fluid around the thylakoids in a chloroplast, where the Calvin cycle happens.
  • ATP · NADPH: Energy carrier and electron carrier made by the light-dependent reactions.
  • Rubisco: The enzyme that joins CO₂ to RuBP (carbon fixation).
  • RuBP: Ribulose 1,5-bisphosphate: a 5-carbon sugar that accepts CO₂.
  • 3-PGA: 3-phosphoglycerate: the first stable 3-carbon product of fixation.
  • G3P: Glyceraldehyde 3-phosphate: a 3-carbon sugar used to make glucose.
  • C4 · CAM: Alternative pathways that help plants in hot, dry climates save water.

Differentiation

Foundation ★

  1. State where the Calvin cycle happens, its inputs and its main product.
Answers: Answer
The stroma of the chloroplast. Inputs: CO₂, ATP and NADPH. Product: G3P, used to make glucose.

Secure ★★

  1. Describe the three phases for 3 CO₂, including the numbers of ATP and NADPH.
Answers: Answer
Fix: 3 CO₂ + 3 RuBP → 6 3-PGA. Reduce: 6 ATP + 6 NADPH → 6 G3P (1 leaves). Regenerate: 5 G3P + 3 ATP → 3 RuBP.

Advanced ★★★

  1. Trace an electron from water to glucose.
  2. Explain why CAM helps desert plants.
Answers: Answers
1. H₂O → PSII → ETC → PSI → NADPH → G3P → glucose.
2. CAM opens stomata at night, storing CO₂, so less water is lost in the hot day.
