Encounter: safe or danger zone?

Tap a sound to sort it: safe, or in the danger zone (85 dB+)? Tap again to change.

Why? Sounds above about 85 dB have a high intensity: enough energy to damage the hair cells in your ear. Each +10 dB sounds about twice as loud.
Distance matters: further away, the intensity is lower and the sound is safer.

Intensity (the unconventional version)

Think of it like this

Think of a can of spray paint. Up close it makes a small, bold spot. From far away, the same paint spreads into a big, faint patch. Sound energy spreads out the same way, so each square metre gets less.

Textbook definition

The intensity of a sound is the sound energy passing through 1 m² each second.

How the wave shape changes

When the source gives a wave more energy, the amplitude grows: taller crests, deeper troughs. The wavelength stays the same.

A harder drum beat makes taller waves on a graph and denser compressions in the air. Think of the drums at an Al Ayyala performance on National Day.

Same wave, new shape
Rain on a wadi pool: drop a raindrop and watch both leaves.
Both leaves sit on the dashed rest line.
They shrink! As each ripple spreads into a bigger circle, its energy is shared out, so its amplitude gets smaller. That is intensity falling with distance.

Loudness needs a medium

A sound wave carries its energy through matter. A louder source sends bigger compressions through the medium. Tap Send: in a vacuum, even a huge bang reaches you as silence.

Orange · At level

  1. Draw a quiet and a loud sound as transverse waves with the same wavelength. Label the amplitude.
  2. Why does the adhan sound quieter from a mosque far away?
  3. Draw a quiet and a loud sound as compressions and rarefactions.
Answers
1. Same distance crest to crest; the loud wave has much taller crests and deeper troughs.
2. The energy spreads over a bigger area as it travels, so intensity and amplitude fall.
3. Loud: tightly packed compressions, very spread rarefactions. Quiet: particles only slightly bunched.

Purple · Challenge

  2. Dubai Airport ground crews wear ear defenders. Explain using intensity and decibels.

Answers
1. About 4 times as loud: +20 dB is two doublings (2 × 2).
2. Jets close up are about 130 dB, deep in the danger zone. That high intensity can damage the ear; defenders absorb the energy.
3. Change: distance (0.5, 1, 2, 3 m). Keep: speaker, volume, sound, room. Measure: dB on a sound-meter app, 3 times each.

Mystery solved!

Why Shamma heard soft pops

Each firework made the air vibrate with a huge amplitude. As the sound travelled 3 km, its energy SPREAD OUT over a bigger and bigger area, so the INTENSITY fell. Lower intensity meant a smaller AMPLITUDE when it reached Shamma, so the same bangs sounded much quieter to her than to Hamdan at the fountain.
