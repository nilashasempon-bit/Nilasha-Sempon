Success criteria

  • I can explain that more energy at the source gives a bigger amplitude and a louder sound.
  • I can define intensity and explain why it decreases as sound travels away from its source.
  • I can use the decibel scale to compare loudness and identify sounds in the danger zone.
  • I can draw how the shape of transverse and longitudinal waves changes as amplitude changes.

Key vocabulary

  • Amplitude: The maximum distance a wave moves from its rest position.
  • Intensity: The sound energy passing through 1 m² each second.
  • Loudness: How you perceive the energy of a sound wave.
  • Decibel (dB): The unit that describes intensity and loudness.
  • Compression: Where particles in a sound wave are squeezed together.
  • Rarefaction: Where particles in a sound wave are spread apart.
  • E ∝ A²: Energy is proportional to amplitude squared: 2× amplitude = 4× energy.
  • Danger zone: About 85 dB and above: can cause permanent hearing loss.

Differentiation

Green · Support

  1. Complete: “Loudness is measured in ____.”
  2. Circle: walking away from a speaker makes it louder / quieter.
  3. True or false: a loud sound has a bigger amplitude than a soft one.
  4. Which is louder: a whisper (30 dB) or a lawn mower (90 dB)?
Answers: Answers
1. decibels (dB)
2. quieter
3. True
4. the lawn mower

Orange · At level

  1. Draw a quiet and a loud sound as transverse waves with the same wavelength. Label the amplitude.
  2. Why does the adhan sound quieter from a mosque far away?
  3. Draw a quiet and a loud sound as compressions and rarefactions.
Answers: Answers
1. Same distance crest to crest; the loud wave has much taller crests and deeper troughs.
2. The energy spreads over a bigger area as it travels, so intensity and amplitude fall.
3. Loud: tightly packed compressions, very spread rarefactions. Quiet: particles only slightly bunched.

Purple · Challenge

  1. Each +10 dB sounds about twice as loud. How much louder does 90 dB seem than 70 dB?
  2. Dubai Airport ground crews wear ear defenders. Explain using intensity and decibels.
  3. Design a test: how does distance from a speaker affect the dB reading? Change? Keep? Measure?
Answers: Answers
1. About 4 times as loud: +20 dB is two doublings (2 × 2).
2. Jets close up are about 130 dB, deep in the danger zone. That high intensity can damage the ear; defenders absorb the energy.
3. Change: distance (0.5, 1, 2, 3 m). Keep: speaker, volume, sound, room. Measure: dB on a sound-meter app, 3 times each.
